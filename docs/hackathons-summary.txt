Hackathons and Competitions

Harshita has actively participated in hackathons and enjoys building under pressure. Her two most impactful hackathon experiences were Innerve 9.0 and Smart India Hackathon 2025, where she led the team in both.

INNERVE 9.0 — SKYLINK (Team Lead + AI/ML Role)

At Innerve 9.0, Harshita's team built Skylink, a real-time in-flight communication platform designed to improve passenger interaction before, during, and after flights. The core problem was that air travel feels isolated — passengers have no simple way to communicate with others on the same flight, receive collaborative updates, or interact during delays.

The platform had two communication modes:
- Online real-time chatrooms using Supabase Realtime features
- Offline peer-to-peer messaging using WebRTC-based local network communication

Additional features: privacy-focused nickname-based access, AI-moderated chat safety, live flight updates, and a Gemini API-powered chatbot assistant inside the platform.

Harshita's role: Team lead and AI/ML side. She built the AI moderation pipeline using BERT/PyTorch-based text classification to detect offensive or abusive messages inside chats. She also worked on ensuring the moderation pipeline integrated without introducing noticeable delays to the real-time chat flow.

Why BERT for moderation: Moderation is a classification problem, not a generation problem. BERT-style transformer models are better suited for reliably identifying unsafe content than generative models.

Hardest technical challenge: Integrating offline peer-to-peer communication and online real-time communication inside the same unified chat interface.

Result: Skylink won the sponsored problem statement by udChalo at Innerve 9.0. This win directly led to Harshita's Software Engineering internship at udChalo.

SMART INDIA HACKATHON 2025 — ZEROGRID (Team Lead + AI/ML Role)

At Smart India Hackathon 2025, Harshita's team built ZeroGrid, a renewable energy orchestration platform for government institutes in Rajasthan. Harshita was the team leader and won the hackathon.

The problem: many institutes had renewable infrastructure (solar panels, batteries, wind systems) but these systems operated independently without intelligent coordination — causing inefficient energy usage, poor forecasting, and limited optimization.

The platform:
- Real-time energy monitoring dashboards
- WebSocket-based live updates across all systems
- Forecasting systems for energy generation and load prediction
- AI-generated operational insights
- Battery usage optimization
- Device integrations through MQTT/Modbus-style flows
- Coordination across solar, wind, battery, and grid systems

Harshita's role: Team lead and AI/ML intelligence layer. She worked on:
- Forecasting workflows and integrating prediction systems
- AI-generated insight pipelines
- Connecting AI outputs with operational decisions (battery scheduling, energy optimization)
- Ensuring the ML system was not treated as an isolated module but integrated into the broader orchestration decisions

Hardest technical challenge: Coordinating multiple moving pieces — forecasting systems, real-time WebSocket updates, dashboards, and optimization workflows — within a hackathon timeline.

GENERAL HACKATHON APPROACH

How Harshita divides work as team lead: She assigns ownership of complete workflows rather than isolated tasks. Instead of splitting random pages, she gives teammates ownership of entire flows like authentication, real-time messaging, or AI integration. This reduces coordination confusion and makes debugging easier.

How she handles unclear requirements: She builds a minimal working flow first instead of overplanning. Once a basic system works, discussions become concrete and iteration gets faster. Momentum and rapid feedback loops matter more than waiting for perfect clarity.

Failure mode learned: Overbuilding features too quickly creates integration chaos. Underestimating real-time integration and debugging time — especially with multiple teammates on interconnected systems — caused friction. Lesson: stabilize core workflows before expanding features aggressively.

Fastest technology learned: Real-time communication workflows involving WebSockets and streaming systems — handling live updates, async communication, and synchronization across multiple users and services under time pressure.
